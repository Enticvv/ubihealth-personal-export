Privacy policy

Effective November 5, 2026

This policy covers the personal Google Health-to-Box export pilot operated by Jude Welsch under the configured app name “UbiHealth Lab.” It does not establish a participant study, public service, or institution-wide data policy.

1. Purpose and scope

The exporter retrieves authorized records from the operator’s Google Health account and saves an organized archive in the operator’s designated Box folder. The current pilot is for one person’s own accounts. This information website does not provide an enrollment form or directly retrieve health records.

2. Information accessed

Google sign-in supplies account identity information, including the email address and account identifier needed to check that the intended account is connected. With permission, the exporter reads the configured Google Health data collections:

  • Activity and fitness records, including steps, distance, exercise, activity levels, energy estimates, and available VO₂ metrics.
  • Sleep and health measurements, including heart rate, heart-rate variability, oxygen saturation, breathing rate, temperature, body measurements, and blood-glucose records when available.
  • Nutrition and hydration records.
  • Electrocardiogram records and irregular-rhythm notifications when supported and authorized.

The export can also include timestamps, record identifiers, source information, and other metadata returned with these records. Not every account or device supplies every category. These are API-provided records and summaries, not a promise of access to raw wearable signals.

The application retains OAuth credentials and tokens needed to perform future authorized exports. It does not ask for the user’s Google or Box password. The Box connection identifies the destination account and permits uploading and managing export files. Its OAuth grant may allow access beyond the chosen folder; the exporter is configured to operate on its designated export destination.

3. Use of information

Information is used to authenticate the intended accounts, read authorized records, organize dated exports, verify file delivery, prevent overlapping runs, and diagnose export failures. The exporter does not sell health records, serve advertisements, or train general-purpose AI models. Any later home-sensing analysis, participant study, or different use requires its own applicable review and disclosures before it is introduced.

4. Storage and service providers

  • Google Cloud: runs the export job. Secret Manager stores authentication credentials. Cloud Storage keeps checkpoints, lock information, and operational state; job logs and run reports contain operational metadata.
  • Box: stores compressed data files, manifests, and execution summaries in the selected export folder. Separate historical pilot folders or copies may also remain until removed.
  • Website hosting: serves this informational website and may process ordinary connection information, such as IP addresses and request logs. No health files or OAuth tokens are embedded in this website.

Access is controlled by the permissions on the Google Cloud project and Box account or folders. People with those permissions, including relevant service administrators, may be able to access stored information. Exporting does not make records anonymous. Google and Box also handle information under their own service terms and policies.

5. Retention and deletion

The exporter retains saved records until they are manually removed. It can save a new snapshot when records change, while preserving previous files. Rechecking recent dates is not a seven-day deletion policy. Backups, older export folders, operational reports, and previously downloaded or shared copies may contain additional retained information.

To stop collection, pause the scheduled job and revoke the app’s connections in the Google and Box account settings. Revocation stops future authorized access but does not erase files already exported. Deletion also requires removing applicable Box exports, retained copies, cloud state, and stored credentials as appropriate. Provider trash, backup, logging, and institutional retention rules may affect when data is fully removed.

6. Security and user choices

The application uses OAuth, HTTPS service requests, and cloud access controls. The scheduled worker stores its tokens in Secret Manager rather than in this website. These measures do not guarantee absolute security. The operator is responsible for account access, folder sharing, credential management, and reviewing permissions.

Google access can be reviewed or revoked at Google Account connections. Box authorization can be managed through the Box account’s connected-application settings. Questions about the exporter or requests about retained copies can be sent to judewelsch@gmail.com.

7. Google user data and policy changes

The app’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will follow the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Changes to collected data or its purpose must be reflected in these disclosures and any required authorization before the new behavior begins. A future participant-facing service is outside this personal pilot’s current scope.
